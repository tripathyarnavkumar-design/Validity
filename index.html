How it’s judged
  • α and ω: ≥ .90 excellent, .80 good, .70 acceptable, .60 questionable, below .60 poor; above .95 may mean redundant items
  • Two-item scales: report the Spearman–Brown coefficient instead of α
  • KR-20 is α for items scored 0/1
  • Corrected item–total r ≥ .30; a negative value usually means an item needs reverse-coding
  • Mean inter-item r between .15 and .50
α  = k/(k−1) · (1 − Σσᵢ² ÷ σₓ²)
ω  = (Σλ)² ÷ [(Σλ)² + Σ(1 − λ²)]
SB = 2r ÷ (1 + r)   (odd vs even items)

Cronbach (1951); McDonald (1999); Kuder & Richardson (1937); Eisinga, te Grotenhuis & Pelzer (2013); George & Mallery (2003); Clark & Watson (1995).

How it’s judged
  • ICC: below .50 poor, .50–.75 moderate, .75–.90 good, above .90 excellent
  • ICC(A,1), absolute agreement, is the usual test–retest coefficient; ICC(C,1) ignores a uniform shift between occasions
  • Pearson r shows association but not agreement; report it with an ICC
  • Weighted κ for coded items with seven or fewer levels
  • At least 30 people measured twice
ICC(A,1) = (MSR − MSE) ÷ [MSR + (k−1)MSE + k(MSC − MSE)/n]
ICC(C,1) = (MSR − MSE) ÷ [MSR + (k−1)MSE]

Shrout & Fleiss (1979); McGraw & Wong (1996); Koo & Li (2016); Weir (2005).

How it’s judged
  • Categories: Cohen’s κ (two raters) or Fleiss’ κ (three or more). Landis & Koch: .41–.60 moderate, .61–.80 substantial, above .80 almost perfect
  • Ordered categories: weighted κ (two raters) or Krippendorff’s α (ordinal)
  • Scores: ICC. Use ICC(A,1) if one rater’s score will be used, ICC(A,k) if scores are averaged
  • Krippendorff’s α ≥ .80 reliable, .667–.80 for tentative conclusions only; it handles missing ratings
  • Intra-rater reliability uses the same coefficients, with one rater’s occasions as the columns
κ = (pₒ − pₑ) ÷ (1 − pₑ)
α = 1 − Dₒ ÷ Dₑ
W = 12S ÷ [m²(n³ − n) − mΣT]

Cohen (1960, 1968); Fleiss (1971); Landis & Koch (1977); Krippendorff (2004, 2011); Kendall & Babington Smith (1939); Koo & Li (2016).

    Can the effect be attributed to the cause you studied, rather than to something else?
    
      
    
      
    
      
    How it’s judged
    
        
    • Score ≥ 75% with no applicable threat left unaddressed
    • Baseline difference |g| ≤ .05 equivalent; .05–.25 adjust statistically; above .25 not equivalent
    • Report overall and differential attrition
    
        
    Score = Σ ratings ÷ (2 × rated threats)
g = (M₁ − M₂) ÷ s_pooled · J
    
        
    Ratings are your judgement. Justify each one in the text.

    External validity review
    
        
    Will the findings hold for other people, settings, treatments and times?
    
      
    
      
    
      
    How it’s judged
    
        
    • Sample does not differ from the population: p ≥ .05 and Cohen’s w below .30
    • No category with a standardised residual beyond ±2
    • Expected count of at least 5 in every category
    • Generalisability review score ≥ 75%
    
        
    χ² = Σ (O − E)² ÷ E,  df = k − 1
w  = √(χ² ÷ n)
    
        
    Bracht & Glass (1968); Shadish, Cook & Campbell (2002); Cohen (1988).
